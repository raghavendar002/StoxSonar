What the columns mean
Type
Breakout: the price just rose out of a sideways range. Breakdown: it just fell out of one. A breakdown is a warning to avoid or exit the stock, not a tip to short it.
Grade
How strong the stock's momentum was at the breakout, by its score. A is the top fifth of past breakouts, B the top half. C and below are not shown. In a Weak market every buy grade is cut one level, and no buys are suggested at all.
Score
Average of four momentum measures: RS rank, 6-month return, distance above the 200-day average and how far above the base it closed. 0 is a typical past breakout.
Buy
How many shares risk 0.5% of Rs 100,000 if the stop is hit. Buy at the next open, and skip it if the open is above the limit shown (1 ATR above the close).
Stop
Sell if it closes below this, measured from the signal close: 2.5 average daily ranges.
Sell by
Time exit: sell at the close 21 trading days after the signal (approximate; exchange holidays move it later).
Fail line
Same level as the stop: a close beyond it marks the signal Failed.
Stage at signal
The stock's trend when the signal fired. Advancing (Stage 2) is the uptrend breakouts should come from. Basing is flat after a fall, Topping is flat after a rise, Declining is a downtrend.
Template
How many of 8 trend checks pass (averages in the right order, near the 52-week high, stronger than most stocks). 8/8 is a clean uptrend.
RS rank
Relative strength: how this stock moved over the past year compared with all others, 1 to 99. 90 means it beat 90% of NSE stocks.
Base
The sideways range it broke out of: how many trading days it lasted and how deep it was from top to bottom.
Volume
Shares traded on the signal day compared with a normal day. 2x or more shows real buying.
Signal date
The trading day the signal fired, after that day's close.
Status
Active: still near the breakout level, where you risk the least if you are wrong. Extended: already more than 5% above it, so buying now means chasing. Confirmed: held above the level for 10 days. Failed: closed beyond its fail line. Faded: dipped below and never recovered or failed.
Move since
How much the stock has moved since the signal day's close.

How it works

Stage follows Weinstein: the 150-day average and its one-month slope decide Basing, Advancing, Topping or Declining, and a change counts only after 5 sessions. Template is Minervini's 8-point trend template, shown for context. Breakout is a close at least 2% above a base of 15+ sessions and 8–35% depth, on 1.5x average volume, closing in the upper half of the day with a small upper wick. Score averages four measures of momentum: RS rank, the 6-month return, distance above the 200-day average and how far above the base the stock closed. Only breakouts above the 200-day average can grade A or B: A is a score in the top fifth of 2006–2018 breakouts, B the top half. A signal is Confirmed if it holds above the base for 10 sessions and Failed if it closes below its fail line, 2.5 average daily ranges under the breakout close. Results are measured from the next day's open, the first price anyone could buy at. R is the 21-session move divided by the distance to the fail line.
